# Proof Reel — Director's Package

**Title:** One Brief. Two Clocks.
**Length:** 60 seconds master, plus 30s and 15s cutdowns and 9:16 versions.
**Job:** In one minute, show a buyer that DWIM delivers a full package faster and cheaper, with a senior human in charge. This reel is the proof asset for every outreach message in `LAUNCH.md`.

## 1. The idea

One brief goes to two clocks. The left clock is the traditional way. The right clock is DWIM. The right clock stops hours in. The left clock is still running. Then the deliverables wall fills the screen, and a senior producer signs off on it. The reel proves speed and craft in the same minute.

**The rule that makes it work: every claim on screen must be true.**
- The DWIM side is a **real run, timed on screen**, from a real brief, using the real pipeline. Show it sped up and label it "timelapse."
- The traditional side is either **your own real past job** (best: you produced and timed it) or a labeled **"typical schedule"** from your 30 years of experience. Label it clearly. Do not present an estimate as a measurement.
- No stock footage presented as ours. No client names or logos without written permission. No City of Aventura branding. Set Lower Third Studio to a neutral DWIM brand before rendering.

Deliverables:

Source: **your own on-camera interview** (see section 6). Using your own footage means no rights or likeness questions.

## 5. Look and sound

- **Palette:** ink black and white with one tally red, matching the site. Red only on the clocks and the end card.
- **Type:** the site's display face for titles, a monospace for clocks and labels.
- **Clocks:** big, flat, tabular numerals. Left clock in grey, right clock in red. They are the hero of the piece.
- **Motion:** hard cuts on beats, quick wipes between tiles, no filler transitions. Everything that moves earns its place.
- **Music:** about 100–110 BPM, minimal and driving, licensed. Build to the 0:24 drop. Pick from Artlist's licensed catalog. Keep the license on file.
- **Sound design:** clock ticks, soft keystrokes on the right side, a single low hit at the freeze.

## 8. Pipeline (how we make it in the style we sell)

1. Ingest interview and screen captures into a Resolve project.
2. Transcribe and mark soundbites. Assemble the 60s hero cut.
3. Cut the six versions from the same timeline, reframe to 9:16, 1:1 and 4:5.
4. Render lower thirds and drop them on the timelines.
5. Burn captions and export an SRT. Check spelling and timing by hand.
6. Loudness-normalize: −14 LUFS for social, −24 LKFS for a broadcast master.
7. QC pass by you. Export. Archive the project and a rights log.
